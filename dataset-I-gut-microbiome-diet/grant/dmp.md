# Data management plan

**Project:** GutGrain: how wholegrain barley changes the gut microbiome of healthy adults
**Applicant:** Dr Helen Marsh, School of Biosciences, Nidderdale University (fictional)
**Funder:** BBSRC responsive mode, 36 months. Reference on award: BB/Z999990/1 (fictional)
**Version:** 1.0, submitted with the proposal (June 2024). Not updated since.

## 1. Data areas and data types

We will recruit 60 healthy adult volunteers for a 12-week dietary intervention. The project will generate shotgun metagenomic sequences from 360 stool samples (about 2 TB of FASTQ files), targeted metabolite measurements from the same samples (about 5 GB), and food diaries and health questionnaires for each volunteer (about 200 MB). Samples and records will be labelled with study codes; the key linking codes to names will be kept separately by the study nurse.

## 2. Standards and metadata

Sequence data will be described using the Genomic Standards Consortium MIxS human-gut checklist. Metabolite data will follow the Metabolomics Standards Initiative reporting guidelines. Each dataset will carry a README file describing collection, processing steps and software versions, so that a secondary user can understand its origin and manipulation.

## 3. Relationship to other data

Sequences will be compared with public reference genomes from the European Nucleotide Archive and the Unified Human Gastrointestinal Genome collection. These resources are openly licensed and place no restrictions on our outputs.

## 4. Secondary use

The datasets will be useful to researchers studying diet and microbiome interactions, to developers of metagenomic analysis methods, and for meta-analyses across dietary intervention studies.

## 5. Methods for data sharing

All data, including sequence data, metabolite data and questionnaire responses, will be made openly available to the research community. Data will be posted on the project website, and researchers may also request copies by email from the principal investigator. Volunteers will sign the University's standard consent form, which states that their samples and data will be used by the GutGrain research team for the purposes of this study.

## 6. Proprietary data

The project has no commercial partner and uses no third-party proprietary data. We do not expect to generate patentable results, so no restrictions on sharing are needed for intellectual property reasons.

## 7. Timeframes

Sequence and metabolite data will be released when the main findings are published, and in any case no later than three years after each dataset is generated.

## 8. Format of the final dataset

Sequence data will be held as compressed FASTQ files. Metabolite tables, food diaries and questionnaire responses will be held as CSV files with an accompanying data dictionary.

## Resources

Data will be stored on the University's research data store during the project, with nightly back-up to a second site. We request £9,000 to pay the annual fees for commercial cloud storage of the datasets, invoiced each year after the grant has ended, and £4,000 for a data manager's time to prepare the data for release during the final six months of the project.
