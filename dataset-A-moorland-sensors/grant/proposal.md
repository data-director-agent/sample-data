## Vision

Upland peat restoration is meant to raise water tables, cool the near-surface microclimate and buffer ecosystems against summer heat. Most of the evidence comes from a few long-term sites with heavy instrumentation. MICROMOOR will test whether **low-cost logger networks** can resolve elevation-dependent microclimate and soil moisture gradients at the resolution land managers need, on a moor in its third year after restoration.

The project will produce an open, well-documented year-long dataset that other groups can reuse to calibrate their own low-cost networks.

## Approach

**WP1: Network deployment (months 1 to 6).** We will install **three** solar-powered logger nodes (N01 to N03) along an elevation transect from about 310 m to 410 m on privately owned moorland, under an access agreement with the landowner. Each node will log air temperature, relative humidity, soil temperature at 5 cm, soil moisture and particulate matter (PM2.5) every 30 minutes.

**WP2: Monitoring year (months 6 to 18).** Continuous logging for 12 months, with monthly site visits for maintenance and download. *If the first season shows a clear gradient, we may extend the transect upslope with up to three further nodes, subject to the budget.*

**WP3: Analysis and synthesis (months 15 to 30).** Lapse rates, soil moisture response to rainfall events, and comparison with a commercial reference weather station (see Resources).

**Risks.** Equipment loss to weather or livestock (mitigation: spares budget). Logger firmware issues (mitigation: field-test before deployment). Access withdrawn by the landowner (mitigation: written agreement in place before installation).
