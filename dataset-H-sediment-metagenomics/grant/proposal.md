## Vision

Treated wastewater carries antibiotic residues, resistant bacteria and mobile genetic elements into rivers. Sediments may store and amplify resistance genes long after the water has passed. HOLME-AMR will quantify antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes in river-bed sediment above and below a wastewater treatment works on a single **UK river**, using shotgun metagenomics, and build an open, well-annotated reference dataset for comparison with other catchments.

## Approach

**WP1: Sampling (months 1 to 9).** Surface sediment (0 to 5 cm) from an upstream (reference) site and a downstream site below the outfall on the River Holme, in replicate, over two visits.

**WP2: Sequencing and bioinformatics (months 9 to 24).** DNA extraction, shotgun sequencing (paired-end 150 bp) at the Holmebridge Genomics Facility, taxonomic profiling and AMR gene annotation against a public resistance-gene database, in a containerised workflow.

**WP3: Synthesis (months 20 to 36).** Upstream/downstream comparison; links to the water company's published discharge records.

*Future work, not costed: comparison with catchments in other countries through collaborators, subject to further funding.*

**Risks.** Low DNA yield from sandy sediment (mitigation: pilot extraction). Flood events preventing sampling (mitigation: flexible visit dates).
