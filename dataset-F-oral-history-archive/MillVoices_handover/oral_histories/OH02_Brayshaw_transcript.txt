MILL VOICES ORAL HISTORY PROJECT
Interview OH02
Interviewee: Walter Brayshaw (b. 1938)
Interviewer: Nadia Pell (NP)
Date: 29 January 2025. Place: Netherbrook Library meeting room.
Duration: 1 h 28 min. Transcript: full, lightly edited. Checked by NP. TEI version in tei/.

[00:00:20]
NP: Walter, you were an overlooker. Could you explain what that meant?

WB: Overlooker, tackler, depends where you were. I kept the looms running. Every weaver had four, six, eight looms, and when one went wrong they put their hand up and I came. Belts, pickers, shuttles, the lot. You were paid on what the weavers made, so if you were slow they lost money and so did you. You learnt quick.

[00:09:55]
NP: Where did you work?

WB: Brook Lane from 1953, then Holroyd's across the valley from 1966 until it closed. Holroyd's was bigger, more modern, but harder. The new management came in from Manchester and they wanted figures, figures, figures.

[00:24:10]
NP: You mentioned the union earlier.

WB: I was in the union all my working life, like my dad. Most of us were. It wasn't anything dramatic most of the time, it was holiday pay and the canteen and getting the guards fixed on the looms.

NP: And 1971?

WB: Aye, 1971. They wanted to cut the rates and put us on more looms each. We came out for nine weeks. It was a cold winter and it was hard on families. I was on the picket most mornings. The photographer from the Advertiser came up and took us all stood at the gate. I've never seen that picture since, until you showed me.

[00:38:42]
NP: What happened after the strike?

WB: Some of us went back and some of us didn't get taken back. That's the bit that still makes me angry. Dennis Pickersgill, he was our shop steward, lovely man, he still lives up on Moor Road, you'll see him at the bowls club. After the strike the firms round here passed his name between them. He never got another job in a mill. Never. He had a scuffle with a lorry driver on the picket line, it was nothing, and they made it into a court case and he did six months. Everybody in Netherbrook knows that story but nobody says it out loud, because of his wife. She never got over it.

NP: Do you want me to keep that in the recording?

WB: Keep it in. It's true. Somebody should say it.

[00:55:03]
NP: How did the closure of Holroyd's affect you?

WB: I was forty-four. Too old to start again and too young to stop. I did odd jobs, maintenance at the school. I missed the shed. I missed being needed. When a loom stopped, it was me they wanted. You don't get that feeling back.

[01:20:30]
NP: Is there anything you'd like to add?

WB: Only that we were skilled men and women. Write that down. Put my name to it, I'm not ashamed of any of it.

[END OF TRANSCRIPT]
