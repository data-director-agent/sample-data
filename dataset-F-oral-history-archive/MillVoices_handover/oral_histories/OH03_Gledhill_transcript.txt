MILL VOICES ORAL HISTORY PROJECT
Interview OH03
Interviewee: Maureen Gledhill (b. 1946)
Interviewer: Nadia Pell (NP)
Date: 12 February 2025. Place: interviewee's home, Netherbrook.
Duration: 1 h 01 min. Transcript: full. Not yet checked.

[00:00:15]
NP: You worked as a mender. What did that involve?

MG: When the cloth came off the loom it came to us, the menders, and we went over every inch on a frame by the window. Any broken thread, any fault, we'd mend it with a needle so you couldn't see. It was fine work. Good eyesight and a steady hand. I loved it, I'll be honest.

[00:12:30]
NP: What was the atmosphere like in the mending room?

MG: Quiet, compared to the shed. You could talk. We knew everything about everybody. Who was courting, who was expecting, who'd fallen out. Some of it I wouldn't repeat even now.

[00:21:47]
NP: You said before we started that some things were difficult.

MG: There was one of the under-managers. I won't say his name. He'd come into the mending room when the forewoman was out. He'd stand too close. Everybody knew. Some of the girls left because of him. I told my mother and she said keep your head down, you need the job. So I did. I've never told my husband that, not in fifty years.

NP: Thank you for telling me. We can talk about how this part is kept.

MG: I want it kept. I want it on record somewhere. But not now. Not while he's alive, or his family round here. Not on any computer that anybody can look at.

[00:40:05]
NP: Tell me about the Whitsun walks.

MG: Oh, lovely. All the chapels walked through the town with their banners and the children in new clothes. That's me and my cousins in the photograph, I'd be about eighteen. The little ones at the front are my cousin's children and the Holroyd twins. They'll be in their sixties now.

[00:58:10]
NP: Anything else?

MG: Just that I'm glad somebody's asking. But you'll keep to what's on the form, won't you? I ticked the closed box for a reason.

[END OF TRANSCRIPT]
