[00:00:30]
NP: Frank, you started in the dyehouse?

FS: Dyehouse at Holroyd's, nineteen fifty. Fifteen years old. You came home blue one week and red the next. My mother used to make me strip in the yard before I came in the house.

[00:08:12]
NP: What was the work like?

FS: Hot, wet and dangerous. The vats, the steam. Nobody wore gloves to start with. There were chemicals in there we didn't know the names of. We'd find out later. A lot of the dyehouse men didn't make old bones. I'm one of the lucky ones.

[00:19:40]
NP: Did you stay in the dyehouse?

FS: Twenty-nine years. Became the foreman. When they closed it I had the best part of thirty years of knowing how to get a colour exactly right, and nobody wanted it. That's what closure is. It's not the building. It's what's in your head that nobody wants.
