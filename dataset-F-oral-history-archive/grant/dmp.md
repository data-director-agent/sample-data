# Data Management Plan (AHRC)

**Project:** Mill Voices: work, community and memory in the Netherbrook mills, 1950 to 1980
**Principal investigator:** Dr R. Ackroyd, Wharfedale University (fictional)
**Version:** 1.0, submitted with the application (March 2024). Not since revised.

## 1. Data summary

The project will create: about twelve oral-history interviews (audio, WAV 48 kHz/24-bit, about 1.5 GB per hour, plus MP3 access copies), full transcripts (plain text, with TEI XML for selected interviews), about 60 digitised photographs lent by residents (TIFF masters, PNG access copies), about 20 new photographs of mill buildings by a commissioned photographer, and exhibition texts. Total about 40 GB.

## 2. Data collection

Interviews will be recorded by the research associate on a University-owned recorder and transcribed in full. Photographs will be scanned at 600 dpi. File names follow the pattern `OHnn` for interviews and `IMG_nnnn` for scans. A catalogue spreadsheet will record the source and rights of every image.

## 3. Short-term data storage

All data, including audio masters, will be kept on the University's secure research storage, which is backed up nightly. No project data will be kept only on personal devices.

## 4. Long-term data storage

At the end of the project, audio masters, transcripts and the image archive will be deposited in the University's DSpace research repository and a copy of the recordings and transcripts offered to the British Library's oral history collections through National Life Stories. The project website will be maintained by the University for at least ten years.

## 5. Data sharing

All interviewees will sign a recording agreement **assigning copyright in the recording to the University** and permitting open access. Transcripts and audio will be made openly available under CC BY 4.0. The project will only digitise and publish photographs where the lender has cleared them for open online use. Data will be shared at the end of the project.

## 6. Ethical and legal considerations

Interviewees will be named, with their consent, as is usual practice in oral history. Interviews deal with working lives and are not expected to contain sensitive personal data. Ethical approval will be sought from the Faculty of Arts research ethics committee. The research associate is responsible for data management.
