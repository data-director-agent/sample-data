## Vision

The textile mills of the Netherbrook valley closed within living memory, but the people who worked in them are now in their eighties and nineties. Their knowledge of skilled work, workplace community and industrial decline is largely unrecorded. Mill Voices will record that knowledge before it is lost, and return it to the town through an exhibition, a website and a permanent archive.

## Approach

**Strand 1: Oral histories (months 1 to 12).** The research associate will record **about twelve** life-story interviews with former mill workers, recruited through Netherbrook Library and local chapels. Interviews follow Oral History Society good practice. Each interviewee signs a recording agreement after the interview, choosing open or closed access.

**Strand 2: Photographs (months 3 to 15).** Residents will be invited to lend family photographs for digitisation at a community scanning day. Originals will be returned to the lenders. A professional photographer will document the surviving mill buildings.

**Strand 3: Outputs (months 12 to 24).** An exhibition at Netherbrook Library, a project website with audio clips (maintained by the University for at least ten years), a journal article, and deposit of the archive.

## Ethics

The project will be reviewed by the Faculty of Arts research ethics committee. Interviewees are older adults; the interviewer will be trained in sensitive interviewing. Personal data are limited to interviewees' names and life stories, shared with their consent.
