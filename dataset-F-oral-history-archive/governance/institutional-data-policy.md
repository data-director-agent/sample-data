# Research Data Management Policy

**Wharfedale University (fictional)**
**Version 2.1, approved by University Research Committee, 9 May 2024.**

## 1. Scope

This policy applies to research data created or collected by staff and students of the University, including audio, images and text created in arts and humanities research.

## 2. Storage

2.1 Research data must be stored on University-managed storage while the project is active. Personal devices and personal cloud or hosting accounts must not be the only place where research data are held.

2.2 When a member of staff who manages research data leaves, the principal investigator must make sure all project data are transferred to University storage before their last day.

## 3. Personal data

3.1 Oral histories and other recordings of identifiable people contain personal data. Data about third parties mentioned in a recording are personal data too, even when the interviewee consents to being named.

3.2 Special category data (including trade union membership, health, and sex life) and data about criminal convictions must be reviewed by the Data Protection Officer before any publication or deposit.

## 4. Rights

4.1 Principal investigators are responsible for clearing the rights in material they publish or deposit, including third-party copyright. The **Library Copyright Officer** advises on rights, orphan works and licences.

4.2 Interviewees' recording agreements and lenders' loan agreements must be kept with the project records for as long as the data are kept.

## 5. Sharing and deposit

5.1 Research data underpinning a funded project must be deposited in the University's research repository (which runs on DSpace) or a suitable discipline, national or community archive, with access in line with the rights and agreements that apply.

5.2 Before any deposit of recordings of people, the **Research Data Steward** reviews the agreements and, with the PI, decides which items may be open, which closed or embargoed, and which withheld.

5.3 A national, local authority or community archive, such as the British Library's oral history collections, may be a suitable home for oral histories and local photographs, alongside or instead of the University repository, provided the agreements with interviewees and lenders allow it.

## 6. Contacts
