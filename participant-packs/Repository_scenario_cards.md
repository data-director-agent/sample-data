# Repository recommendation: scenario cards (participant version)

Pick **one** card. Ask an AI tool where the data should be deposited, then check its answer against the real sources: re3data or FAIRsharing, the funder's data policy, your own institution's policy, and the journal policy printed on the card.

The researchers, journals and projects on these cards are **fictional**. The funders and repositories are **real**, so you can check what the tool tells you.

**Suggested prompt (edit as you like):**
> I need to deposit this dataset. Recommend a ranked shortlist of repositories with reasons and any deposit fees. Tell me where my funder, journal and institution requirements clash, and say what you are not sure about.

Each card contains at least one built-in conflict. Did the tool find it?

---

## Card 1: Upland sensor data (pairs with Dataset A)

- **You are:** an environmental scientist depositing a year of microclimate sensor data (two CSV files, about 6 MB) for a paper.
- **Funder:** NERC (UK Research and Innovation).
- **Journal (fictional):** *Journal of Upland Ecology*. Policy: *"Data underpinning the results must be deposited in a publicly accessible repository that issues a DOI, and that DOI cited in the Data Availability statement. Data must be available at publication under CC BY 4.0 or CC0."*
- **Institution:** your own, with its own repository and policy.
- **Also true:** the dataset was collected on private land. One sensor sits close to a protected bird's nest site. A commercial weather series was used for comparison.
- **Budget for deposit fees:** none.

## Card 2: Materials data with an industrial partner (pairs with Dataset B)

- **You are:** a materials physicist depositing X-ray diffraction patterns and electron microscope images for a paper (about 3 MB in total, plus one proprietary instrument file).
- **Funder:** EPSRC.
- **Journal (fictional):** *Journal of Emerging Photonic Materials*. Policy: *"Raw and processed data must be deposited in an open repository with a persistent identifier before acceptance. Restrictions are allowed only with a stated justification."*
- **Institution:** your own.
- **Also true:** some of the samples were made from precursors supplied by an industrial partner. The collaboration agreement gives the partner a 90-day review period before publication. A patent filing is under discussion.
- **Budget for deposit fees:** none.

## Card 3: Community survey (pairs with Dataset C)

- **You are:** a social scientist depositing an online survey of 312 adults (one table plus documents, about 100 KB).
- **Funder:** ESRC.
- **Journal (fictional):** *Journal of Community Health Studies*. Policy: *"Authors must make anonymised data available to reviewers on request and deposit them in a trusted repository on publication."*
- **Institution:** your own.
- **Also true:** the survey asked about health, ethnicity and gender identity. The participants consented under a form that you have not yet re-read closely.
- **Budget for deposit fees:** none.

## Card 4: Computational study with large outputs (pairs with Dataset D)

- **You are:** a doctoral researcher depositing a flow-simulation study: code, inputs, logs and result tables (about 30 MB), plus **2.1 TB** of field output on scratch storage.
- **Funder:** EPSRC.
- **Journal (fictional):** *Computational Fluid Dynamics Letters*. Policy: *"Source code and the data needed to reproduce the figures must be deposited with a DOI. Datasets larger than 50 GB should be described, and a representative subset deposited."*
- **Institution:** your own.
- **Also true:** the code was adapted from an open-source project. A commercial mesh file was used. The project ended and the scratch storage will be cleaned.
- **Budget for deposit fees:** none.

## Card 5: Crystallography (stands alone)

- **You are:** a chemist depositing the single-crystal X-ray results for a new coordination polymer: a CIF, a reflection file, and about 18 GB of raw diffraction images (several thousand frames).
- **Funder:** EPSRC.
- **Journal (fictional):** *Crystal Engineering Communications*. Policy: *"Crystal structure CIFs must be deposited with the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre, and the deposition number cited. Raw diffraction images should be deposited where feasible."*
- **Institution:** your own.
- **Budget for deposit fees:** none.

## After you have the answer

Record on the scratchpad rubric:
1. Did the tool find the conflict? Which one?
2. Did it invent a repository, a policy or a fee?
3. How long did checking take, compared with doing it yourself?
4. At what point should a person have to sign off the choice?
